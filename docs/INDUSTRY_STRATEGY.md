# Industry strategy — which demos to build, and in what order

The demos are sales tools. Each new one should either open a market Embark Digitals can sell into, or cost
little because it reuses a family that already exists. This page records the reasoning so future additions
are chosen, not stumbled on.

## How to judge a candidate industry

Score each candidate from 1 to 3 on five questions:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Pain**: is the work run on paper, WhatsApp, spreadsheets or a dated desktop system? | Visible pain makes a demo persuasive |
| **Process**: does work move through clear stages with rules (approvals, expiries, compliance)? | Workflows and guards are what this foundation does best |
| **Money**: can a 5–50 person business pay a monthly fee, and is there billing to streamline? | Sustainable recurring revenue |
| **Reach**: are there many such businesses within reach in South Africa, especially SMEs? | Size of the pipeline |
| **Reuse**: how much of an existing family carries over? | Cost and speed of the demo |

## Families (how the demos are organised)

| Family | Built | Shared shape | Cheap next members |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health practices | Optometry, General practice | Diary → clinical record → script/Rx → medical-aid claim → recall | Dental, physiotherapy, veterinary, audiology, psychology |
| Vehicles & workshops | Automotive workshop, Tyre & fitment centre | Job card through bays → customer approval → parts/tools → QC → invoice | Panel beaters (insurance assessor approvals), fleet maintenance, auto electricians |
| Site & field operations | Mining, Construction | Daily log → permits/safety → plant → contractor compliance → progress claims | Security companies (guard rosters, incidents), cleaning & facilities, electrical/plumbing contractors |
| Property & professional services | Real estate, Law firm | Pipeline of deals/matters → compliance checks → trust money → fees | Property management & body corporates (levies, arrears, maintenance), accounting & bookkeeping practices, insurance brokers |

## Recommended next demos (in order)

1. **Property management / body corporate (sectional title & HOA).** Levies, arrears, owner and tenant records,
   maintenance, AGM and trustee decisions. High pain, recurring billing, many estates and complexes, and it reuses
   real estate (rentals, maintenance) and legal (trust-style ledger).
2. **Dental practice.** Nearly all of optometry and GP carries over (diary, charting instead of refraction, claims,
   recalls). Very cheap and a large, well-paid market.
3. **Private schools and early-learning centres.** Enrolments, fee billing and arrears, attendance, parent
   communication. Strong pain and recurring fees. It is a new family, so it costs more, but it opens the education sector.
4. **Security companies.** Guard scheduling, site patrols, incident reports, PSIRA registration expiries. Reuses
   mining/construction (shifts, incidents, compliance expiries).
5. **Panel beaters.** Insurance-driven jobs (assessor approval, parts authorisation, photos at each stage). Reuses the
   workshop family almost entirely and is a common client profile.
6. **Accounting & bookkeeping practices.** Client deadlines (VAT, PAYE, provisional tax), document requests, time and
   fees. Reuses legal (deadlines, time, fee notes).

Lower priority for now: pharmacies (heavily regulated, entrenched vendors), large hospitality groups (crowded,
integration-heavy), agriculture (fragmented needs). Revisit if a specific client appears.

## How to use the demos in sales

- Lead with the family slide: "We have already built this pattern for practices like yours". Then show the specific
  industry link.
- Send each prospect only their own client link (`docs/SHARING_LINKS.md`); keep the hub for internal use and
  presentations.
- Use the "Questions to ask a prospective client" in `docs/BUSINESS_SCOPE_AND_LIMITATIONS.md` to turn the demo into a
  scoped proposal.

These rankings are a judgement based on how the foundation works and the general shape of the South African SME
market. They are not market research; validate them with conversations with real prospects before investing heavily.
